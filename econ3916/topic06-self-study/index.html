Answer these in writing — about 15 minutes

Six of the slides are questions I would have put to the room. The deck reveals each answer on the next click, which makes them nearly worthless if you click first. Write your answer down before you click. A committed wrong answer teaches you something; a nod does not.

1. You need the share of Northeastern undergraduates with a paid internship lined up. Which sample do you want?

A) 8,000 replies to an Instagram story poll   B) 300 replies from a random draw off the registrar’s list   C) 8,000 replies to a survey emailed to every student   D) 300 replies from the students in this room

Then write one sentence on why C is the tempting one.

2. Name the crescent.

I estimate the average rent paid by Boston residents by surveying everyone in our classroom. (a) Name the target population, the frame, and the sample. (b) Name one group that is in the population but structurally absent from my frame. (c) Does their absence push my rent estimate up or down?

Part (c) is the one that matters. Commit to a direction — a signed guess beats a shrug.

3. An estimate’s error has three ingredients: how bent the selection is, how variable the outcome is, and how much of the population you missed. You collect ten times more data from the same source. Which shrinks?

A) All three   B) How bent the selection is   C) How much you missed, and only that   D) None

4. “How much do Boston renters pay?” Rank these best to worst, and say what breaks in your worst one.

(a) 4,000,000 Zillow listing views   (b) 1,200 randomly dialled Boston phone numbers   (c) every lease filed with the city last year   (d) 50,000 Reddit r/boston comments mentioning rent

Be ready to defend your last place on Monday. This one is genuinely arguable and I want the argument.

5. Across 100 runs the biased estimate’s errors have a standard deviation of 0.01 points; the random estimate’s is 0.20. The biased one is twenty times more stable. Why is that bad news?

A) It is good news   B) Stability only reflects the bigger sample and says nothing about being right   C) The random sample must have a bug   D) Low variance implies low bias

6. You are predicting which customers churn next month. Which of these leaks?

A) months_as_customer, recorded at signup   B) fitting StandardScaler on train, applying to test   C) tickets_closed_after_cancellation   D) splitting 80/20 with stratify=y

Two of these four are the correct procedure. Say which, and why they are fine.

Part 2 — due Friday 9 OctoberProblem Set 1

A client thinks the CPI overstates what their workers actually face. You build a price index for one household that you invent, compare it against the official series, and hand back a finding your director could forward without editing. There is no right household, so there is nothing to look up.

Do the warm-up notebook first. It is not graded, and it is the difference between a smooth Phase 1 and a stalled one. Its four sections are the four things Phases 1 to 3 ask of you, in the same order: write a function, build a list of dictionaries and loop over it, read one series from FRED, find the months that are missing.

The AI rule

Prohibited in Phases 1 to 3. Required in Phase 4. Colab may explain an error message or explain a line to you — the same rule as the labs. It may not write or fix your code.

A Phase 4 submitted with no transcript is graded as though the AI work had not been done. I would rather you read that here than discover it at the grade.

If you cannot explain a line you submitted, you cannot submit it.

The two parts with no lab behind them

Everything else in this problem set has a worked example in a lab. These two did not, so I added them:

  • Rebasing to 2019 = 100. Now worked, with its output, in Lab 2, Part 2, Step 4 (continued). The arithmetic is one line: divide by the base period’s average, multiply by 100.
  • The monthly grid. asfreq("MS") appears in the warm-up §4 and in Lab 2, Step 1b. Without it you will count missing values on a series that has already dropped its gaps, get zero, and report that nothing is missing. Phase 3.1 is built to catch exactly that.

Lab 2 Step 1b prints the count of missing months, not which month. That is deliberate — naming the month, working out why it is missing and saying what you would do about it is Phase 3.1, and it is yours to find.
