The Big Through-Line of Test 2

One sentence

Every firm — perfect competitor, monopolist, monopolistic competitor, or oligopolist — maximizes profit the same way: produce where MR = MC. What changes between structures is what MR looks like and what happens in the long run.

What does MR look like?

Price taker (perfect competition): demand is flat, so MR = P.
Anyone with market power (monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly): demand slopes down, so MR < P — that’s why they restrict output.

What happens in the long run?

Free entry drives economic profit to zero under perfect and monopolistic competition.
Barriers to entry let a monopolist (and often an oligopolist) keep economic profit.

If you internalize that one idea, Chapters 10–13 stop being four chapters and become four variations on a single theme. The master comparison table near the end of this deck is the single most testable slide — build it from memory.

Q1. Which of the following is an implicit cost for Maria, a software engineer who quit a $150,000 job to run her own bakery?

A) The $42,000 rent she pays for the storefront

B) The $85,000 she spends on flour, sugar, and ingredients

C) The $150,000 salary she gave up to run the bakery

Q2. Campus Coffee has fixed costs of $25/hour. With 2 baristas it makes 30 drinks at a total cost of $100/hr; with 3 baristas it makes 42 drinks at a total cost of $133/hr. The marginal cost of the drinks added by the third barista is about:

A) $2.75 per drink

B) $3.17 per drink

C) $4.44 per drink

D) $9.00 per drink

Answer

A

A) Fixed costs are minimized at that output

B) While the next unit costs less than the running average it pulls ATC down, and once it costs more it pulls ATC up, so they must be equal at the turning point

Perfect Competition · Practice MC #4

Q4. A pizza shop produces where P = MC. At that quantity P = $8, ATC = $9.50, AVC = $7. In the short run the firm should:

A) Shut down, because it is losing money

B) Shut down, because P < ATC

C) Keep operating, because P > AVC, so it covers variable cost and offsets some fixed cost

D) Raise its price to $9.50 to break even

Answer

C

Compare P to AVC for the open/close call: P $8 > AVC $7, so keep operating and minimize the loss. The loss of $1.50/unit beats shutting down and eating the full fixed cost. (A price-taker can’t "raise its price" — that’s why D is wrong.)

Perfect Competition · Practice Problem 10.A: Clara’s Bagel Cart

Set-up Clara is a price-taker; FC = $60/day. (Q, TC, ATC, AVC): (10, 140, 14.00, 8.00), (20, 200, 10.00, 7.00), (30, 270, 9.00, 7.00), (40, 360, 9.00, 7.50), (50, 480, 9.60, 8.40). MC between rows: $6, $7, $9, $12. (a) At P = $12, find Q*. (b) Profit/loss = (P − ATC)×Q. (c) At P = $7, operate or shut down? (d) Long-run if profit is positive at $12?

Answer Sketch

(a) Walk up the MC column at P = $12: $6, $7, $9 all below $12, and the 40→50 step costs $12 = P. So Q* = 50 dozen (P = MC on the last step). (b) At Q = 50, ATC = $9.60. Profit = ($12 − $9.60) × 50 = $2.40 × 50 = $120 profit. (Check: TR $600 − TC $480 = $120.) (c) At P = $7: min AVC = $7, so P = min AVC — right at the shutdown margin. Operating loses exactly her FC ($60); shutting down also loses $60 → she’s indifferent. A cent below $7 (P < AVC) → shut down. (d) Free entry competes the $120 profit away; supply shifts right and P falls to min ATC = $9 (zero economic profit) — the invisible hand.

A) the monopolist always charges the highest price on the demand curve

B) selling an extra unit requires lowering the price on all units sold, so the price effect drags revenue down

C) marginal cost rises as output increases

Monopoly · Practice Problem 11.A: The Only Pharmacy in Town

Set-up Maple Pharmacy is a local monopoly; constant MC = $8 (ATC ≈ MC). Demand: P = $18→Q1, $16→Q2, $14→Q3, $12→Q4, $10→Q5, $8→Q6. (a) Build TR and MR. (b) Profit-max Q and P (largest Q with MR ≥ MC). (c) Compare to competition (P = MC = $8). (d) Who gains, who loses?

Answer Sketch

(a) TR = P×Q; MR (per unit) = $18, $14, $10, $6, $2, −$2... — MR falls twice as fast as price. (b) Largest Q with MR ≥ MC = $8: MR is $10 at Q = 3 (≥ 8) but $6 at Q = 4 (< 8). Gainer: the monopolist. Losers: consumers + society (DWL).

Monopolistic Competition · Practice Problem 12.A: The Crowded Coffee Block

Set-up Brew & Bytes sells Q = 300 drinks at P = $5.00, with ATC = $4.00 and MC = $3.20. (a) Daily economic profit? (b) How does its demand curve shift, and why? (c) Long-run outcome (P, ATC, profit, markup, excess capacity)? (d) Productively efficient? Is the variety worth it?

Answer Sketch

(a) Profit = (P − ATC)×Q = ($5.00 − $4.00) × 300 = $300/day (positive). Q3. A car wash sells each wash for $8. The third worker raises total daily washes from 40 to 52. Workers are paid $120/day. Regarding the third worker, the firm should:

A) hire — MRP ($96) is below the wage

B) not hire — MRP ($96) is below the wage ($120)

C) hire — MRP ($416) exceeds the wage

D) not hire — MRP equals the wage exactly

Answer

B

MP = 52 − 40 = 12 washes. MRP = MP × P = 12 × $8 = $96. Compare to the $120 wage: $96 < $120, so don’t hire that worker. (C wrongly uses total product 52 × $8.)

A

Capital & Land · Practice Problem 15.A: Should the Bakery Buy the Oven?

Set-up A new oven costs $50,000 and generates extra profit of $22,000 (Yr 1), $24,200 (Yr 2), $26,620 (Yr 3), then no resale value. Interest rate r = 10%. (a) PV of each year. (b) Total PV. (c) Buy it? State the rule. (d) If r rose to 15%, more or less likely to buy?

Set-up Slice processes $400,000 in gross transaction value and keeps $92,000 in total platform revenue. Students pay $0 in delivery fees (launch promo); restaurants pay a 20% commission. (a) Overall take rate? (b) Which side is subsidized vs. monetized, and why? (c) Will the market "tip"? (d) How does a data flywheel help Slice stay on top?

Top 10 Mistakes to Avoid

1

Accounting vs. economic profit. Economic profit also subtracts implicit costs (forgone salary, forgone interest). Positive accounting profit can hide negative economic profit.

2

Shutdown uses AVC, not ATC. In the short run, keep producing while P ≥ AVC (it still helps cover fixed costs). Exit in the long run if P < ATC.

3

MR ≠ P for anyone with market power. Only the perfect competitor has MR = P. Monopoly, monopolistic comp., and oligopoly all have MR < P — that’s why they restrict output.

4

Profit-max is MR = MC, never "where profit per unit is biggest." Maximize total profit, then read price off the demand curve (for price-setters).

5

Mon. comp. earns zero LR profit but is still inefficient. Tangency gives zero profit, yet P > MC and the firm has excess capacity. Zero profit ≠ efficient.

6

Nash equilibrium ≠ the best joint outcome. In a prisoner’s dilemma the stable (Nash) cell is worse for both than cooperating. Don’t pick the cooperative cell as the equilibrium.

7

Economies of scale ≠ diminishing returns. Scale is long-run (all inputs vary, LRAC); diminishing returns is short-run (one input fixed, MP). Different horizons, different curves.

8

MRP hiring compares MRP to the wage — not total revenue product to total wages. Hire the last worker whose MRP ≥ wage.

9

Present value discounts each year separately. $100 in year 2 at 10% is 100/(1.10)² = $82.64, not 100/(1.10×2). Don’t forget the exponent.

10

Take rate is a share, not a dollar amount. $12 on a $50 order is a 24% take rate — the fee divided by the transaction value.
